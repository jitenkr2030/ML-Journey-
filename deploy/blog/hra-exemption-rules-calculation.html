HRA Exemption Rules & Calculation 2026-27 — Complete Guide

January 21, 20267 min readBy ReconcileAI Team

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is one of the biggest tax-saving components for salaried employees in India. If you live in a rented house, you can claim HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act. This guide explains the complete HRA exemption rules, calculation method, and common mistakes for FY 2026-27.

What is HRA Exemption?

HRA is a component of your salary paid by your employer to cover rental expenses. Under Section 10(13A) read with Rule 2A, you can claim tax exemption on HRA received, subject to certain conditions and limits.

Key Point
HRA exemption is available only under the old tax regime. If you opt for the new tax regime (default), you cannot claim HRA exemption. Calculate both regimes to see which saves more tax.

HRA Exemption Calculation (3 Conditions)

HRA exemption is the LEAST of these three amounts:

ConditionFormulaNotes
1. Actual HRA receivedAs per salary slipAnnual HRA component
2. Rent minus 10% of salaryAnnual Rent − 10% of (Basic + DA)If negative, HRA = 0
3. 50% of salary (Metro)50% of (Basic + DA)Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai
3. 40% of salary (Non-Metro)40% of (Basic + DA)All other cities

HRA Calculation Example

Example 1: Metro City (Mumbai)

ComponentAmount (Annual)
Basic Salary + DA₹6,00,000
HRA Received₹2,40,000
Actual Rent Paid₹3,00,000

Calculation:

  1. Actual HRA received: ₹2,40,000
  2. Rent − 10% of salary: ₹3,00,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹2,40,000
  3. 50% of salary (Metro): 50% × ₹6,00,000 = ₹3,00,000

HRA Exemption = Least of (₹2,40,000, ₹2,40,000, ₹3,00,000) = ₹2,40,000

Taxable HRA = ₹2,40,000 − ₹2,40,000 = ₹0 (Fully exempt!)

Example 2: Non-Metro City (Pune)

ComponentAmount (Annual)
Basic Salary + DA₹4,80,000
HRA Received₹1,92,000
Actual Rent Paid₹1,80,000

Calculation:

  1. Actual HRA received: ₹1,92,000
  2. Rent − 10% of salary: ₹1,80,000 − ₹48,000 = ₹1,32,000
  3. 40% of salary (Non-Metro): 40% × ₹4,80,000 = ₹1,92,000

HRA Exemption = Least of (₹1,92,000, ₹1,32,000, ₹1,92,000) = ₹1,32,000

Taxable HRA = ₹1,92,000 − ₹1,32,000 = ₹60,000

Documents Required for HRA Claim

  • Rent receipts: Monthly rent receipts with landlord name, address, amount, period
  • Rent agreement: Registered lease/rent agreement
  • Landlord PAN: Mandatory if annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000
  • Bank statement: Showing rent payment (for cash payments, keep receipts)
  • Landlord declaration: If landlord has no PAN, obtain a declaration
Landlord PAN Mandatory!
If total rent paid exceeds ₹1,00,000 per year (₹8,333/month), landlord PAN is mandatory. Without it, the HRA claim will be rejected. If landlord is an NRI, deduct TDS at 30% on rent.

HRA Special Cases

Living With Parents

You can claim HRA exemption if you pay rent to your parents. Conditions:

  • Parents own the house
  • Parents declare the rent as income in their ITR
  • Genuine rent payment (bank transfer preferred)
  • Rent agreement between you and parents

Own House + Rented House in Another City

If you own a house in one city but live in a rented house in another (due to work), you can claim HRA exemption on the rented house AND home loan interest on the owned house.

No HRA in Salary

If you don't receive HRA but pay rent, you can claim deduction under Section 80GG: Up to ₹5,000/month (₹60,000/year), subject to conditions.

Common HRA Mistakes

  1. Not keeping rent receipts: CAs and tax officers demand receipts. Digital receipts are acceptable.
  2. Exaggerating rent: Rent should be realistic. Rent of ₹50,000/month for a 1BHK will attract scrutiny.
  3. Missing landlord PAN: Claim gets rejected without PAN for rent above ₹1 lakh/year.
  4. Not declaring landlord income: If landlord doesn't show rental income, both can face notices.
  5. Ignoring 10% salary deduction: Many employees miscalculate. The 10% deduction significantly reduces exemption.
Tax Savings Example
At 30% tax bracket, a ₹2,40,000 HRA exemption saves ₹72,000 in tax (plus cess). Add 80C (₹45,000) and 80D (₹15,000) for total savings of ₹1,32,000 per year! This is why HRA is the #1 tax-saving tool for salaried employees.
