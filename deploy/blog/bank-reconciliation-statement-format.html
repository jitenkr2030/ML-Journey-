Bank Reconciliation Statement Format & Preparation Guide 2026

January 23, 20267 min readBy ReconcileAI Team

A bank reconciliation statement (BRS) is a document that explains the difference between the bank balance shown in your accounting books and the balance shown on the bank statement. This guide covers the complete BRS format, preparation method, and examples for Indian businesses.

What is a Bank Reconciliation Statement?

Bank reconciliation is the process of matching your cash book balance (accounting records) with the bank statement balance. Differences arise due to:

  • Checks issued but not yet cleared
  • Checks deposited but not yet credited
  • Bank charges and interest
  • Direct deposits and withdrawals
  • Errors in books or bank records
  • Standing instructions (EMI, insurance premiums)

Bank Reconciliation Statement Format

Format 1: Starting From Bank Statement Balance

Bank Reconciliation Statement as at [Date] ================================================================ Particulars Amount (₹) ---------------------------------------------------------------- Balance as per Bank Statement XXX,XXX Add: Checks deposited but not yet cleared XX,XXX Add: Direct deposits not recorded in books XX,XXX Add: Interest credited by bank XX,XXX Add: Errors in bank statement (understated) XX,XXX -------- Less: Checks issued but not yet cleared (XX,XXX) Less: Bank charges not recorded in books (XX,XXX) Less: Direct withdrawals (EMI, insurance) (XX,XXX) Less: Errors in bank statement (overstated) (XX,XXX) -------- Adjusted Bank Balance XXX,XXX ================================================================

Format 2: Starting From Cash Book Balance

Bank Reconciliation Statement as at [Date] ================================================================ Particulars Amount (₹) ---------------------------------------------------------------- Balance as per Cash Book XXX,XXX Add: Bank charges not recorded in books XX,XXX Add: Checks issued but not yet cleared XX,XXX Add: Direct withdrawals (EMI, insurance) XX,XXX -------- Less: Checks deposited but not yet cleared (XX,XXX) Less: Direct deposits not recorded in books (XX,XXX) Less: Interest credited by bank (XX,XXX) -------- Adjusted Bank Balance XXX,XXX ================================================================

Step-by-Step Preparation Method

Step 1: Get the Bank Statement

Download or collect the bank statement for the reconciliation period (usually monthly). Most Indian banks provide statements in PDF, Excel, or CSV format via net banking.

Step 2: Get the Cash Book / Ledger

Export the bank ledger from your accounting software (Tally, Busy, Zoho Books). This shows all bank transactions recorded in your books.

Step 3: Match Transactions

Compare each entry in the cash book with the bank statement. Mark matched items. Unmatched items fall into categories:

CategoryDescriptionAdjustment
Checks issued, not clearedPayment recorded in books, not in bankAdd to bank balance
Checks deposited, not clearedReceipt recorded in books, not in bankLess from bank balance
Bank chargesDebited by bank, not in booksLess from book balance
Interest creditedAdded by bank, not in booksAdd to book balance
Direct depositsReceived directly in bank (e.g., NEFT)Add to book balance
Direct withdrawalsEMI, insurance, standing instructionsLess from book balance

Step 4: Prepare the Statement

Using either format above, add/subtract the adjustments to arrive at the matching balance. If both sides don't match, there's an error — recheck your entries.

Step 5: Pass Adjusting Entries

For every item found during reconciliation that isn't in your books, pass a journal entry:

  • Bank charges: Dr Bank Charges, Cr Bank
  • Interest credited: Dr Bank, Cr Interest Income
  • Direct deposit: Dr Bank, Cr Party/Revenue
  • EMI/Insurance debit: Dr Loan/Expense, Cr Bank

Why Bank Reconciliation Matters

  1. Financial accuracy: Ensures your books reflect actual bank position.
  2. Fraud detection: Unauthorized transactions are caught immediately.
  3. Tax compliance: Accurate cash flow data is essential for GST and income tax filing.
  4. Audit readiness: CAs and auditors require monthly reconciliation reports.
  5. Cash flow management: Know your exact cash position for business decisions.
