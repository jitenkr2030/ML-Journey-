Capital Gains Tax on Property Sale 2026 — Complete Guide

January 22, 20268 min readBy ReconcileAI Team

Selling a property in India triggers capital gains tax. The tax depends on how long you held the property (short-term vs long-term), the sale price, and the cost of acquisition. This guide covers the complete capital gains tax on property sale for FY 2026-27.

Short-Term vs Long-Term Capital Gains

FeatureShort-Term (STCG)Long-Term (LTCG)
Holding periodLess than 24 months24 months or more
Tax rateSlab rates (up to 30%)12.5% (with indexation) or 20% (without indexation)
Indexation benefit❌ Not available✅ Available (Cost Inflation Index)
Exemptions availableLimited54, 54B, 54EC, 54F
Budget 2024 Change
From FY 2024-25, LTCG on property is taxed at 12.5% without indexation or 20% with indexation, whichever is lower. This replaced the earlier flat 20% with indexation. You can choose whichever option benefits you.

How to Calculate Capital Gains

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)

STCG = Sale Price − Cost of Acquisition − Cost of Improvement − Selling Expenses

Tax = STCG × Applicable slab rate (up to 30% + cess)

Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)

LTCG = Sale Price − Indexed Cost of Acquisition − Indexed Cost of Improvement − Selling Expenses Indexed Cost = Actual Cost × (CII of year of sale / CII of year of purchase)

Capital Gains Tax Example

ComponentAmount
Property purchased in 2010₹25,00,000
Property sold in 2026₹80,00,000
Cost of improvement (2015)₹5,00,000
Selling expenses (broker, legal)₹1,50,000
CII 2010-11: 167 | CII 2015-16: 254 | CII 2026-27: 376

With Indexation (20% rate):

  • Indexed cost of acquisition = ₹25,00,000 × (376/167) = ₹56,28,742
  • Indexed cost of improvement = ₹5,00,000 × (376/254) = ₹7,40,157
  • LTCG = ₹80,00,000 − ₹56,28,742 − ₹7,40,157 − ₹1,50,000 = ₹14,81,101
  • Tax @ 20% = ₹2,96,220

Without Indexation (12.5% rate):

  • LTCG = ₹80,00,000 − ₹25,00,000 − ₹5,00,000 − ₹1,50,000 = ₹48,50,000
  • Tax @ 12.5% = ₹6,06,250

Winner: With indexation (lower tax of ₹2,96,220 vs ₹6,06,250)

Note: Compare both options. For long holding periods, indexation usually gives better results. For recent purchases, 12.5% without indexation may be better.

How to Save Capital Gains Tax

Section 54: Reinvest in Residential Property

  • Buy a new residential property within 1 year before or 2 years after sale
  • Or construct a new house within 3 years from sale date
  • Exemption: Full LTCG amount (up to cost of new property)
  • If new property cost is less than LTCG, only partial exemption

Section 54EC: Invest in Capital Gains Bonds

  • Invest in NHAI or REC bonds within 6 months of sale
  • Maximum investment: ₹50,00,000 per financial year
  • Lock-in: 5 years (3 years for bonds issued before April 2023)
  • Interest: 5.25-5.75% (taxable)

Section 54F: Reinvest in Residential Property (Non-Property Assets)

  • Applicable when selling shares, gold, or other capital assets
  • Invest entire sale proceeds in a residential house
  • Exemption: Proportional to amount invested

Section 54B: Agricultural Land

  • Applicable if you sell agricultural land used for farming
  • Invest in another agricultural land within 2 years
  • Exemption: Full LTCG amount
Tax Planning Strategy
If your LTCG is ₹40 lakh and you invest in a new house worth ₹40 lakh under Section 54, you pay zero capital gains tax. This is the most powerful tax-saving tool for property sellers. Plan BEFORE selling — once the sale happens, you have limited time to reinvest.

Note: TDS is on the sale price, not on the capital gain. If sale price is ₹80 lakh, TDS = ₹80,000 (1%). The seller can claim credit in ITR.

Common Mistakes in Capital Gains Calculation

  1. Not using indexation: For properties held 10+ years, indexation dramatically reduces tax.
  2. Missing improvement costs: Renovation, construction, and improvement costs can be indexed and deducted.
  3. Not claiming selling expenses: Brokerage, legal fees, stamp duty on sale are deductible.
  4. Forgetting Section 54: Many sellers don't know they can avoid ALL tax by reinvesting.
  5. Not filing TDS credit: TDS deducted on property sale must be claimed in ITR. Form 26AS shows the credit.
